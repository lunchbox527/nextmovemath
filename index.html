The 2026 Canadian Mortgage Stress Test (+2% OSFI Rules Explained)

Under federal OSFI Guideline B-20, Canadian chartered banks must stress test every mortgage applicant at the qualifying rate: the higher of 5.25% OR your negotiated contract interest rate plus 2.00%. Even if you secure a 4.69% mortgage, your household income must prove you can comfortably afford payments at 6.69%.

The stress test evaluates two fundamental ratios: Gross Debt Service (GDS) and Total Debt Service (TDS). GDS caps housing costs (mortgage payment at the qualifying rate, annual property taxes, heating costs, and 50% of condo fees) at 39% of gross household income. TDS caps total debt obligations (housing plus car loans, student debt, and credit cards) at 44% of gross income.

For example, on a $600,000 mortgage at a 4.89% contract rate, the actual monthly payment is $3,452. However, at the stress test qualifying rate of 6.89%, the monthly payment evaluated by the underwriter is $4,161. This $709 monthly spread requires an additional $21,800 in annual gross household income to pass the bank qualification hurdle.

The Quiet Closing Costs That Catch Home Buyers Off Guard

Your down payment is only part of the cash required on closing day. Quiet closing costs typically add between 1.5% and 3.5% of the purchase price in out-of-pocket expenses before keys are handed over:

  • Land Transfer Taxes (PTT / Transfer Stamps): In British Columbia, Property Transfer Tax is 1% on the first $200,000 and 2% on the balance up to $2,000,000. In Ontario, provincial tax applies, and City of Toronto buyers pay both provincial and municipal land transfer taxes (a double tax).
  • Legal Fees & Disbursements: Real estate lawyers and notaries charge $1,200 to $2,500 to conduct title searches, prepare mortgage security documents, and register ownership.
  • Title Insurance: Mandatory by most prime lenders, costing $300 to $600 to protect against fraud, survey defects, and unpermitted work.
  • Property Tax & Utility Adjustments: The buyer reimburses the seller on the Statement of Adjustments for any prepaid municipal property taxes, water utilities, and heating oil.
  • Home Inspection & Appraisal: Typically $500 to $1,200 paid during the subject condition removal period.

Mortgage Porting vs. Breaking: How to Avoid a $15,000 Bank Penalty

When selling a home before your mortgage term concludes, breaking a fixed-rate mortgage can trigger a punitive Interest Rate Differential (IRD) penalty of $10,000 to $30,000. For variable mortgages, the standard penalty is 3 months of interest.

Mortgage porting allows homeowners to transfer their existing contract interest rate, remaining term, and outstanding balance directly to the new property without penalty. If upsizing, lenders provide a "Blend and Extend" option, blending the existing low rate with prevailing market rates for the additional funds needed.

Selling & Buying Simultaneously: Net Proceeds & Bridge Financing Math

Gross home equity is not the same as cash in the bank. True net proceeds subtract realtor commissions (typically 3% to 5% plus sales tax), legal fees, mortgage discharges, and staging costs.

When closing dates do not align, lenders arrange a Bridge Loan to cover the down payment on the new home using the firm sale contract of the existing home as collateral.
